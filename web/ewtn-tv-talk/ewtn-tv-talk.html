02 The bet

03 Challenges

Where the cost went

  1. Focus handling No mouse or touch screen. The remote's D-pad drives focus. Each OS's native engine decides where it lands.
  2. Screen readers VoiceOver, TalkBack and VoiceView increase the test matrix and fail in opposite ways.
  3. A single codebase for three platforms tvOS, Android Amazon devices and Android Google devices differ.
  4. Poor documentation Far less material on TV apps than on mobile or web, on top of a fork that is little known.
  5. Emulators vs physical devices Focus, accessibility, and performance behave differently on real hardware.

1

Focus handling

On TV there is no mouse or touch screen: focus moves with the remote control's D-pad.

Each OS's native engine decides where focus goes based on the layout's geometry.

The decision: Trust the native engine, supported by react-native-tvos APIs

Recurring symptoms

  • Focus escaping to the side menu during transitions
  • Focus "lost" after asynchronous loads
  • Initial focus on the wrong element
  • Overlapping elements

How we handled it

  • Custom reusable solutions (hooks / wrappers)
  • Preventing focus on the side menu during navigation

2

Screen readers

Screen reader users are about 1% of traffic but they still took a big part of our work because the client requires a strong emphasis on accessibility.

Nonprofit organizations are expected to enhance accessibility on their apps to comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) if they are public accommodations or receive federal funding to avoid litigation.

Focus differentiation

On TV, accessibility-focus differs from interaction focus. The reader announces the element with accessibility-focus.

The two platforms fail in opposite ways

Recurring symptoms

  • Elements not being targeted by accessibility-focus
  • Announced element not matching the focused element
  • Announcements getting cut off when UI updates
  • Elements not being announced on programmatic focus moves

How we handled it

  • Custom reusable solutions (hooks / wrappers) "AccessibleContainer"
  • Pair every focus request with an accessibility-focus request
  • PR policy with evidence videos with the reader on and off
  • Explicit announcements triggered on complex views

3

A single codebase for three platforms: The dark side

The react-native-tvos APIs are meant to simplify and unify expected behaviors such as focus handling or accessibility across platforms.

Fixing one platform issue was prone to breaking another. The codebase may carry many platform-conditional branches to apply different solutions if not handled properly.

Aspect tvOS (Apple TV) Android TV / Google TV Fire TV
Focus engine Infers targets by position and alignment. Supports diagonals and swipe. Looks for the closest element in the pressed direction (up, down, left, right). No diagonals. Same as Android
Event order Blur of the previous element, then focus of the new one. Focus of the new element before blur of the previous one. Same as Android
Screen reader VoiceOver: Cannot land on Text component TalkBack: Lands on Text component VoiceView: Similar to Android
Video player react-native-video does not report bitrate changes react-native-video reports bitrate changes Same as Android

Divergences between applications are isolated in defensive config plugins. They inject native code without maintaining native folders (ios / android)

Advantages

  • One codebase and a single UI for three stores.
  • Expo Router for navigation and EAS for builds and submissions: no local Xcode or Gradle.
  • Usable React Native ecosystem (player, state, validation) and fast iteration with hot reload.
  • The whole team had prior React experience.
  • With the initial idea, the stack would let us reuse a lot of business logic for the future mobile app.

Disadvantages

  • The fork lags behind React Native and Expo. TV support is fragile.
  • Scarce documentation.
  • The native focus engine forces platform conditionals throughout the UI.
  • Complex end-to-end testing.

4

Lack of documentation

react-native-tvos is a fork maintained by few people, with scarce documentation.

Use of AI

AI tools did not contribute much at first. They became useful once a solid base of custom solutions existed for them to use as a reference.

Less information about developing a TV app (and specifically about TV accessibility) than a mobile or web one

5

Emulators vs physical devices

Focus, accessibility, and performance behave differently on real hardware and on emulators.

What only showed up on hardware

  • Apple TV's VoiceOver can only be tested on a physical device.
  • Fire TV has no official emulator. Testing runs over adb against physical sticks, and VoiceView can only be tested on hardware.
  • Between the Android TV emulator and a real Google TV, focus timing and the system keyboard's behavior change.
  • Performance problems, such as D-pad lag or delays when moving between screens, only show up on real hardware.

How we handled it

  • At least one physical device per platform from the start
  • Video evidence per PR, tested on the physical devices

04 Closing

What to take home

The assessment is still positive: today the app is in production, and three stores with a small team.

  1. One app, three stores, a small team. The bet works, with a cost concentrated in focus and accessibility.

  2. The native focus engine and screen readers are the real TV problem. The rest is conventional React Native development.

  3. What upstream does not document, the team documents. In code and in guides.

  4. End-to-end testing with Suitest. To detect any kind of instability in time.
